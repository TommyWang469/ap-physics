# Friction: 5.23-5.41 odd

Source: user-supplied screenshots, textbook pp. 161-163. Statements condensed. g = 9.80 m/s².

Static friction adjusts up to f<sub>s,max</sub> = μ<sub>s</sub>N; sliding friction has magnitude f<sub>k</sub> = μ<sub>k</sub>N. Constant velocity means net force is zero.


## 5.23 Maximum bed tilt


A patient lies on a bed with μ<sub>s</sub> = 1.20 between the patient and sheets. Find the largest angle to the floor before the patient starts sliding.

For the patient: weight mg acts downward, N is perpendicular to the bed, and static friction acts uphill. At the threshold of sliding:

N = mg cos θ; &nbsp; mg sin θ = μ<sub>s</sub>N<br/>tan θ = μ<sub>s</sub> = 1.20

Answer: θ<sub>max</sub> = arctan(1.20) = 50.2°.


## 5.25 Pushing a box, then letting it coast


A 16.8 kg box moves at 3.50 m/s on a level surface with μ<sub>k</sub> = 0.20. (a) Find the horizontal push needed for constant speed. (b) Find its stopping distance after the push is removed.

<b>(a)</b> Vertically, N = mg. Horizontally, constant velocity requires the push to balance kinetic friction.

F = f<sub>k</sub> = μ<sub>k</sub>mg = (0.20)(16.8)(9.80) = 32.928 N

Answer (a): F ≈ 32.9 N in the direction of motion.

<b>(b)</b> After release, only friction acts horizontally. Take the initial motion as positive.

a = −μ<sub>k</sub>g = −1.96 m/s²<br/>v² = v₀² + 2aΔx<br/>0 = (3.50)² − 2(1.96)Δx

Answer (b): Δx = 3.125 m ≈ 3.13 m.

Force directions: N upward; mg downward; friction opposite motion; the applied push is present only in part (a).


## 5.27 Crate of tools


A 45.0 kg crate starts moving when a horizontal push exceeds 313 N; a 208 N push then keeps it moving at 25.0 cm/s. (a) Find μ<sub>s</sub> and μ<sub>k</sub>. (b) Find the push for a = 1.10 m/s². (c) On the Moon (g = 1.62 m/s²), find the starting push and the acceleration with the push from (b).

<b>(a)</b> N = mg = (45.0)(9.80) = 441 N. Use the starting threshold for static friction and the steady push for kinetic friction.

μ<sub>s</sub> = 313/441 = <b>0.710</b>; &nbsp; μ<sub>k</sub> = 208/441 = <b>0.472</b>

<b>(b)</b> Once sliding: F − f<sub>k</sub> = ma, so F = 208 + (45.0)(1.10).

Answer (b): F = 257.5 N ≈ 258 N.

This is the force needed <b>while moving</b>. To start from rest on Earth, first exceed 313 N, then reduce the push to 257.5 N.

<b>(c)</b> Assume the friction coefficients are unchanged on the Moon. Use their unrounded values.

F<sub>start,threshold</sub> = μ<sub>s</sub>mg<sub>Moon</sub> = 313(1.62/9.80) = <b>51.7 N</b><br/>f<sub>k,Moon</sub> = 208(1.62/9.80) = 34.38 N<br/>a = (257.5 − 34.38)/45.0 = <b>4.96 m/s²</b>

A push just above 51.7 N starts it moving; the acceleration is in the direction of the push.


## 5.29 Slide: estimates and general formulas


Estimate a slide’s height and angle. (a) Find the bottom speed from rest with negligible friction. (b) Without wax paper, the speed is half of (a); find μ<sub>k</sub>. (c) A differently dressed child stays at rest; find the minimum μ<sub>s</sub>.

<b>Chosen estimates:</b> vertical height h = 2.0 m and angle θ = 30°. These are assumptions, not values supplied by the problem. Ramp length L = h/sin θ.

<b>(a)</b> With negligible friction, a = g sin θ and v² = 2aL = 2gh.

v = √(2gh) = √[2(9.80)(2.0)] = <b>6.26 m/s</b> (about 6.3 m/s)

<b>(b)</b> With friction, v² = 2gL(sin θ − μ<sub>k</sub> cos θ). Half the original speed means one quarter of its squared speed:

¼ = 1 − μ<sub>k</sub> cot θ ⇒ μ<sub>k</sub> = ¾ tan θ = <b>0.433</b>

<b>(c)</b> Rest requires mg sin θ ≤ μ<sub>s</sub>mg cos θ.

μ<sub>s,min</sub> = tan θ = tan 30° = 0.577.


## 5.31 Push down or up a ramp


A 10.0 kg box slides on a 55.0° ramp with μ<sub>k</sub> = 0.300. A 120.0 N force acts parallel to the ramp. Find the acceleration magnitude for (a) a downhill push and motion; (b) an uphill push and motion.

N = mg cos 55.0° = 56.2 N, so f<sub>k</sub> = 16.86 N. The downhill weight component is mg sin 55.0° = 80.28 N. Friction opposes the stated motion.

<b>(a)</b> Choose downhill positive:<br/>ma = F + mg sin θ − f<sub>k</sub><br/>a = (120.0 + 80.28 − 16.86)/10.0 = <b>18.3 m/s² downhill</b>.

<b>(b)</b> Choose uphill positive:<br/>ma = F − mg sin θ − f<sub>k</sub><br/>a = (120.0 − 80.28 − 16.86)/10.0 = <b>2.29 m/s² uphill</b>.


## 5.33 Stacked boxes moving down a ramp


A 32.0 kg box sits on a 48.0 kg box. Both descend together at 15.0 cm/s, held by a rope parallel to the ramp attached to the lower box. The ramp rises 2.50 m over a horizontal run of 4.75 m; μ<sub>k</sub> = 0.444 at the ramp and μ<sub>s</sub> = 0.800 between boxes. (a) Find the rope force. (b) Find the friction on the upper box.

θ = arctan(2.50/4.75) = 27.76°. Constant velocity means a = 0. The rope contributes one external tension to the two-box system; friction between boxes is internal.

<b>(a)</b> For both boxes together, M = 80.0 kg. Choose uphill positive:

N = Mg cos θ; &nbsp; T + μ<sub>k</sub>Mg cos θ − Mg sin θ = 0<br/>T = Mg(sin θ − μ<sub>k</sub> cos θ) = <b>57.1 N uphill</b>

<b>(b)</b> For the upper box alone, static friction balances its downhill weight component:

f<sub>s</sub> = (32.0)g sin θ = <b>146 N uphill</b><br/>f<sub>s,max</sub> = (0.800)(32.0)g cos θ = 222 N

Because 146 N &lt; 222 N, the upper box can move with the lower box without slipping. Static friction here is 146 N, not its maximum value.


## 5.35 Stopping with locked wheels


(a) With μ<sub>k</sub> = 0.80 on dry pavement, find the stopping distance from 28.7 m/s with locked brakes. (b) With μ<sub>k</sub> = 0.25 on wet pavement, find the initial speed that gives the same distance.

<b>(a)</b> In this sliding-friction model on a level road, N = mg and the acceleration is a = −μ<sub>k</sub>g. The stopping distance follows from 0 = v₀² + 2ad.

d = v₀²/(2μ<sub>k</sub>g) = (28.7)²/[2(0.80)(9.80)]<br/><b>d = 52.5 m</b> (about 53 m)

<b>(b)</b> Set the wet and dry stopping distances equal:

v<sub>wet</sub>²/μ<sub>wet</sub> = v<sub>dry</sub>²/μ<sub>dry</sub><br/>v<sub>wet</sub> = 28.7√(0.25/0.80) = <b>16.0 m/s</b> (about 36 mi/h)

These are braking distances only; reaction distance is excluded. The textbook notes that locking the brakes is not the safest stopping method.


## 5.37 Connected crates on a horizontal floor


Crates A and B have masses m<sub>A</sub> and m<sub>B</sub>, connected by a rope. Both have kinetic-friction coefficient μ<sub>k</sub>. A horizontal force F pulls B to the right at constant velocity. Draw force diagrams and find (a) F and (b) the connecting-rope tension.

On B, the leftward arrow represents two separate forces, T and f<sub>k,B</sub>. Vertically, N<sub>A</sub> = m<sub>A</sub>g and N<sub>B</sub> = m<sub>B</sub>g.

<b>(a)</b> Treat both crates as one system. The rope tension is internal and cancels. With a = 0:

F − μ<sub>k</sub>m<sub>A</sub>g − μ<sub>k</sub>m<sub>B</sub>g = 0

Answer (a): F = μ<sub>k</sub>(m<sub>A</sub> + m<sub>B</sub>)g.

<b>(b)</b> Isolate crate A. Its rightward tension balances its leftward friction:

T − μ<sub>k</sub>m<sub>A</sub>g = 0

Answer (b): T = μ<sub>k</sub>m<sub>A</sub>g.


## 5.39 Two blocks connected over a pulley


Block A (2.25 kg) sits on a table with μ<sub>k</sub> = 0.450. A light cord over a frictionless pulley connects it to hanging block B (1.30 kg). Released from rest, find (a) each speed after moving 3.00 cm and (b) the cord tension.

Assume the blocks begin sliding as the problem intends. Take rightward for A and downward for B as positive. The cord gives both blocks the same acceleration magnitude.

A: T − μ<sub>k</sub>m<sub>A</sub>g = m<sub>A</sub>a<br/>B: m<sub>B</sub>g − T = m<sub>B</sub>a<br/>a = g(m<sub>B</sub> − μ<sub>k</sub>m<sub>A</sub>)/(m<sub>A</sub> + m<sub>B</sub>) = 0.7937 m/s²

<b>(a)</b> v = √(2aΔx) = √[2(0.7937)(0.0300)] = <b>0.218 m/s</b> for each block. A moves toward the pulley; B moves downward.

<b>(b)</b> T = m<sub>B</sub>(g − a) = 1.30(9.80 − 0.7937) = <b>11.7 N</b>.


## 5.41 A downward push increases friction


A crate of mass m has friction coefficients μ<sub>s</sub> and μ<sub>k</sub> on a level floor. A woman pushes at angle θ below horizontal. (a) Find F for constant velocity. (b) Find the critical μ<sub>s</sub> at which no finite push can start it moving.

Forces: weight mg downward; normal N upward; push components F cos θ forward and F sin θ downward; friction backward.

<b>(a)</b> Vertical balance gives N = mg + F sin θ. For steady forward sliding:

F cos θ = μ<sub>k</sub>N = μ<sub>k</sub>(mg + F sin θ)

Answer (a): F = μ<sub>k</sub>mg / (cos θ − μ<sub>k</sub> sin θ).

A positive finite force requires cos θ − μ<sub>k</sub> sin θ &gt; 0; otherwise this push cannot sustain constant forward sliding.

<b>(b)</b> Starting motion requires the horizontal push to exceed maximum static friction:

F cos θ &gt; μ<sub>s</sub>(mg + F sin θ)<br/>F(cos θ − μ<sub>s</sub> sin θ) &gt; μ<sub>s</sub>mg

If μ<sub>s</sub> ≥ cot θ, the left coefficient is nonpositive, so no finite positive F can satisfy the inequality (0° &lt; θ &lt; 90°).

Answer (b): μ<sub>s,critical</sub> = cot θ = 1/tan θ.
